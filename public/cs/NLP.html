Formula sheet

Every formula the recording points at, with the mistake that costs most marks in that row. Learn the right-hand column, not just the formula.

QuantityFormulaMost common mistake
Lexical diversity LDS = |V| / N Counting punctuation as a token. Decide once and say which convention you used.
Heaps' law |V| = k·Nβ, 0.67 < β < 0.75 Reading β as an exact constant. It is a range, and vocabulary grows faster than √N.
Bigram MLE P(wn | wn−1) = C(wn−1wn)C(wn−1) Dividing by the total number of bigrams. The denominator is the count of the first word only.
Chain rule P(w1..n) = Πk P(wk | w1..k−1) Forgetting the first factor P(w1), which has no condition.
Bigram sentence probability P(w1..n) ≈ Πk P(wk | wk−1) Using counts directly instead of the per-word conditional probabilities.
Laplace (add-one) P*(wn | wn−1) = C(wn−1wn) + 1C(wn−1) + V Adding 1 to the denominator instead of V. The denominator grows by the vocabulary size.
Cosine similarity cos(a,b) = a · b‖a‖ ‖b‖ Forgetting the two norms, i.e. reporting the dot product.
HMM forward step αt(j) = [Σi αt−1(i)·aij] · bj(ot) Multiplying the emission term inside the sum. It comes out of the sum once.
Observation likelihood P(o1..T) = Σj αT(j) Summing every column. Only the final column is summed.
Viterbi step vt(j) = maxi[vt−1(i)·aij] · bj(ot) Using Σ instead of max. Summing gives the likelihood, not the best path.
Supervised transition aij = C(i→j) / C(i) Dividing by the total transitions in the corpus rather than by the count of state i.
Supervised emission bj(o) = C(o, j) / C(j) Dividing by the count of the observation instead of the count of the state.
Baum-Welch re-estimation aij = Σt ξt(i,j)Σt γt(i) Using hard counts. Unsupervised EM uses expected counts, ξ and γ.
Posterior state probability γt(i) = αt(i)·βt(i)P(O) Omitting the normalisation by P(O) — γ must sum to 1 over states at each t.
01

Words, instances and vocabulary

The first thing the topic list names. Two definitions, one ratio, one tokenizer.

这一题的起手式

看到一段话让你算 N 和 |V|，按住这个顺序走，不要临场发挥：

  1. 先决定标点算不算 token。算，就在答案里写一句 "punctuation counted as tokens"；不算，就写 "punctuation excluded"。这一句本身就是分。
  2. 全部转小写再切词，这样 The 和 the 才算同一个 type。
  3. N = 数出来的词总数（instances，带重复）； |V| = 去重后的个数（types）。
  4. LDS = |V| / N，除完保留 3–4 位小数，顺手写一句 "the higher the score, the less repetition"。

常见的坑：把 |V| 当成"字典里所有词"。|V| 是这一篇文本里出现过的不同词数。

Model answer — "What are word instances and what is vocabulary size?"

Markstypes vs instances · N is running total · |V| is distinct · LDS · space tokenization

  • 1.1 · 2Instance (also called a token) = one occurrence of a word in running text. N is the total number of instances — every repetition counted.
  • 1.2 · 2Type = a distinct word form. The vocabulary V is the set of all types, and the size of the vocabulary |V| is how many distinct words occur.
  • 1.3 · 2Worked contrast: "the cat sat on the mat" → N = 6, but |V| = 5, because the occurs twice and is counted once.
  • 1.4 · 2Lexical diversity = |V| / N. It falls as a text gets longer; the two limits are 1/N (all words identical) and 1 (no word repeated).
  • 1.5 · 2Heaps' law: |V| = k·Nβ with 0.67 < β < 0.75 — vocabulary grows sub-linearly, faster than √N. A corpus twice as long does not have twice as many distinct words.

Space-based tokenization

Markssplit on whitespace · works for spaced scripts · fails on punctuation, clitics, no-space languages

  • 2.1 · 2Split the string wherever a space character occurs; each run of non-space characters becomes one token. In Python, text.split().
  • 2.2 · 2It is appropriate for languages written with spaces — Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic — and it is the cheapest possible baseline.
  • 2.3 · 2It fails on punctuation glued to words (mat.), multiword expressions (New York), clitics (we're → we + are), and it produces no token at all for languages without spaces (Chinese, Japanese, Thai).
  • 2.4 · 2Consequence: the vocabulary is polluted with mat and mat. as different types, which inflates |V| and splits statistics.

DRILL 1.A

For the sentence below, give N, |V| and LDS. State your punctuation convention.

"The small cat sat on the warm mat and the cat slept"

Show answer

Marksconvention stated · N · |V| · the ratio

  • A · 1No punctuation present, so the convention does not arise.
  • A · 2Tokens (lower-cased): the, small, cat, sat, on, the, warm, mat, and, the, cat, slept → N = 12.
  • A · 2Distinct forms: and, cat, mat, on, sat, slept, small, the, warm → |V| = 9. the appears 3×, cat 2×.
  • A · 1LDS = 9/12 = 0.75.
02

Lemmatization and stemming

Both reduce a word to a shorter form. Only one of them produces a real word.

一定要能当场举例

老师明确说了要"举例，并写出给定单词处理后的结果"。所以别只背定义，把下面这三组对照背下来， 考试直接套：

  • am / is / are / was → lemma be，但 stem 会变成 am / is / ar / wa 之类，很多不是真词。
  • studies → lemma study（真词）； stem studi（不是真词）。这一组最能说明区别。
  • better → lemma good（要查词典才知道）； stem 还是 better（规则砍不掉）。

答题模板：「Lemma 是词典词，要做形态分析、看词性；Stem 是砍前后缀， 不管是不是真词、快但粗糙。」然后立刻跟上例子。

Model answer — "Explain lemmatization and stemming, their difference, and give examples."

Marksdefinition ×2 · dictionary word vs chopped affixes · POS needed · examples · speed/accuracy trade-off

  • 3.1 · 2Lemmatization maps a word form to its lemma — the dictionary headword, i.e. the base form you would look up. It needs a dictionary and the word's part of speech: am, is, are, was → be; cars → car.
  • 3.2 · 2Stemming chops affixes off with rewrite rules and does not care whether the result is a real word: studies → studi, happiness → happi, relational → relat.
  • 3.3 · 2Two differences that carry the marks: (a) a lemma is a real word, a stem need not be; (b) lemmatization is POS-dependent and dictionary-driven, stemming is rule-driven and POS-blind.
  • 3.4 · 2Consequence: stemming is faster and needs no lexicon, so it suits large-scale information retrieval; lemmatization is more accurate, and is preferred when the actual word matters (translation, question answering).
  • 3.5 · 2Both are word normalization: they collapse different surface forms so that car and cars share statistics. Neither can be undone — the information about number and tense is lost.

DRILL 2.A

Give the lemma and a plausible stem for: better, studies, are, mice.

Show answer

Markslemma must be a real word · stem may not be

  • A · 2better → lemma good (irregular; only a dictionary finds this). A Porter-type stemmer leaves it as better, because no suffix rule fires.
  • A · 2studies → lemma study (-ies → -y); stem studi (the rule just strips the s).
  • A · 1are → lemma be; stem typically are (an irregular verb has no suffix to chop).
  • A · 2mice → lemma mouse (suppletive plural — a stemmer cannot handle it at all).
03

Regular expressions

Two things are asked: why they are used, and write one that matches this.

写正则的答题流程

老师举的例子是"写出所有以 le 结尾的单词"。写正则按四步走，写完在草稿纸上 心算两个例子验证：

  1. 先写词边界：\b ... \b。不加边界，led、 sleep 里的 le 也会被匹配到。
  2. 中间填允许的字符集和数量：字母是 [A-Za-z]*；若是"若干个数字"就用 \d{n}。
  3. 把必须出现的字面量放在最后：le。
  4. 双向验证：拿一个应该匹配的、一个不该匹配的，各测一次。这一步很多人跳过，然后丢分。

常见的量词陷阱：? 只管前面一个字符（colou?r 是 color 或 colour）；+ 也是只管前面一个（stars+! 要两个 s）。

The relationship the recording asks you to explain A regular expression and a finite state automaton are two notations for the same regular language, and you can convert in either direction.

One way is Thompson's construction; the other is reading the labels off the accepting paths.

Model answer — "Why do we need regular expressions in NLP?"

Marksformal language for strings · pre-processing role · not a general NLP solution · precision/recall

  • 4.1 · 2A regular expression is a formal language for specifying a set of text strings. It is a pattern, not a program: it describes which strings belong to a language.
  • 4.2 · 2It sits inside bigger systems as a pre-processing and formatting tool — tokenizing, cleaning, extracting emails, dates, prices, hashtags from raw text.
  • 4.3 · 2It is not a general NLP solution. Anything needing semantics — translation, sentiment, question answering — is out of reach of a regex.
  • 4.4 · 2Designing one is a precision/recall trade-off: a broader pattern finds more true cases but adds false positives (there matched by [tT]he); a narrower one misses true cases, adding false negatives.
  • 4.5 · 2In Python use the re module with a raw string (r"\d+"), otherwise \n is read as a newline character rather than "backslash-n".

The three mistakes that lose marks here

  • No anchors or word boundaries. [tT]he also matches inside their. The sentence "The recent attempt by the police to retain their current rates" has three matches for [tT]he but only two for \b[tT]he\b.
  • Misreading a quantifier's scope. ? * + bind to the single preceding character, not to the whole expression. /baa+!/ requires b, then a, then one or more further a, then ! — so ba! is rejected; the shortest accepted string is baa!.
  • Using a greedy quantifier when an exact count is meant. For "exactly two decimal places" write \d{2}, not \d+.

DRILL 3.A

Write a regular expression for each: (a) a hexadecimal colour code #RRGGBB; (b) a date in DD/MM/YYYY form; (c) any word starting with a capital letter followed only by lower-case letters.

Show answer

Marksboundaries · exact counts · character classes

  • A · 3(a) #[0-9A-Fa-f]{6}\b — six hex digits exactly. {6}, not +: #FFF must not match.
  • A · 3(b) \b\d{2}/\d{2}/\d{4}\b — and note that / is not special, so it needs no escape, while a literal . would (\.) because . means "any character".
  • A · 3(c) \b[A-Z][a-z]+\b — the second class is + not * if a single capital letter should not count as a word.

Finite state automata

The recording asks for the relationship in both directions: regex → diagram, and diagram → regex.

画 FSA 的固定套路

老师特意强调"vice versa"，两个方向都要会。画图按这个流程，一定不会错：

  1. 数一数正则里必须按顺序出现的符号，每个符号一个状态。开头加 q0。
  2. 遇到 * 或 +，在那个符号所在的状态上画自环 （箭头绕回自己）。+ 表示至少一次，所以自环之外还要有一条正向的进入边。
  3. 遇到 ?，从同一个状态分两条路，一条经过该符号，一条绕过它。
  4. 最后一个状态画双圈表示接受状态（accepting / final state）。
  5. 反过来"看图写正则"：从 q0 走到双圈，把一路上每条边的标签按顺序读出来， 自环读成 +（或 *），平行分支合成 | 或 ?。

要背的定义五元组：Q（状态集）、Σ（字母表）、 q0（起始状态）、F（接受状态集）、 δ（转移函数）。

The course's own FSA for /baa+!/ Five states, five transitions. q0 is the start state and q4 the accepting state. The a loop drawn on q3 is what the Kleene + looks like as a diagram: one mandatory a plus any number of further ones.

Exam form: “given this regex, draw the diagram” — and the reverse, read the path labels off a diagram and write the regex.

Model answer — "What is the relationship between a regular expression and an FSA?"

Markssame regular language · two notations · 5-tuple definition · both directions · determinism

  • 5.1 · 2A regular expression and a finite state automaton are two notations for exactly the same thing: a regular language. Every regex has an equivalent FSA and every FSA has an equivalent regex.
  • 5.2 · 2Definition to quote — an FSA is a 5-tuple: Q a finite set of states; Σ an alphabet of symbols; q0 the start state; F ⊆ Q the set of accepting states; δ: Q × Σ → Q the transition function. It is a directed graph with labelled nodes and labelled arcs.
  • 5.3 · 2Recognition (acceptance) = start in q0, read the input left to right, follow the arc whose label matches, move to the new state. Accept the string iff you run out of input while standing in a state in F.
  • 5.4 · 2Regex → FSA: build one state per symbol in order, and turn each Kleene operator into a diagram feature — */+ become a self-loop, ? becomes a bypass arc, | becomes a parallel branch. Mark the last state as accepting.
  • 5.5 · 2FSA → regex: read the labels along every path from q0 to a state in F and join them; a self-loop contributes +, alternative paths are joined with |.
  • 5.6 · 2Deterministic means that at every point there is exactly one thing to do — no choices. A table-driven interpreter (D-RECOGNIZE) runs any such machine, and to change the machine you change the table.

FSA for /stars+!/ (Assignment Q8.1) Read the labels along an accepting path: s, t, a, r, then the s loop (one or more), then !. The loop sits on q4 — the state you reach after the first s.

Trap: stars+! needs at least two s characters, so “star!” is rejected.

FSA for /colou?r/ (Assignment Q8.2) The ? makes the u optional, so q3 gains a second outgoing arc and there are two routes into the accepting state q6.

This is an NFA: from q3 on input r you may take the lower arc directly.

FSA for “any word ending in le” The self-loop on q0 consumes any letter; you only commit to the tail once you see an l followed by an e that ends the word.

Regex: \b[A-Za-z]*le\b. The word boundaries are what keep the “le” inside “led” or “sleep” out.

Reading the diagrams backwards is where marks are lost

  • The loop sits on the state reached after the repeated symbol. In /baa+!/ the a loop is on q3 — the state you are in once you have read b a a. Putting it on q2 gives /ba+!/, which accepts a different language.
  • A double ring is not decoration. Only states in F are accepting; a diagram with no double ring accepts nothing.
  • An FSA reads the whole string. Stopping in an accepting state midway is not enough — the input must be exhausted exactly there.
05

Language models and n-grams

What a language model is, why it matters, how unigram / bigram / n-gram differ, and computing a probability from a table of counts.

算 bigram 概率：只要认准分母

老师说要"给你一份简单的词频统计，让你算概率"。所有错误几乎都出在分母上：

  1. 写 P(want | I) 时，先在表里找行 = I、列 = want 的那个格子， 那就是分子 C(I want)。
  2. 分母是行首那个词自己的词频 C(I)，也就是 I 在整个语料里出现多少次。 不是总词数，也不是格子总和。
  3. 算整句概率就把每个 bigram 概率连乘。中间的 <s> 和 </s> 也算一个词，别忘了它们那一步。
  4. 乘出来数字很小是正常的，写成科学计数法；千万不要写成 0。

一句话记住：横着看行、竖着找列、除以行首。

Model answer — "What is a language model and why is it important in NLP?"

Marksassigns probability to sequences · next-word prediction · downstream tasks · LLMs · n-gram limits

  • 6.1 · 2A language model is a system that assigns a probability to a sequence of words, P(w1…wn), and equivalently to an unseen upcoming word, P(wn | w1…wn−1).
  • 6.2 · 2It is important because word prediction is a component of many tasks: spelling and grammar correction (Their are → There are), speech recognition (bassoon dish → back soonish), machine translation, and text generation.
  • 6.3 · 2It is also how large language models work: an autoregressive LLM is trained to predict the next word and generates by predicting the next word over and over.
  • 6.4 · 2Estimation uses the Chain Rule P(w1..n) = Π P(wk | w1..k−1), which is exact but needs probabilities we can never observe — hence the need for an approximation.
  • 6.5 · 2The Markov assumption truncates the history to the last n−1 words: P(wn | w1..n−1) ≈ P(wn | wn−N+1..n−1). That is the whole difference between the model orders below.

Unigram vs bigram vs n-gram — the answer the recording wants

Markshow much context · one table · the trade-off · example

  • 7.1 · 2Unigram: P(w1..n) ≈ Π P(wi). Every word is independent of every other. It ignores word order completely, so it cannot tell the dog bit the man from the man bit the dog.
  • 7.2 · 2Bigram: P(wk | w1..k−1) ≈ P(wk | wk−1). Conditions on one previous word; needs a V × V table of counts.
  • 7.3 · 2n-gram: conditions on the previous n−1 words. Trigram = two words of context, and so on. Larger n means more context and a Vn-sized table.
  • 7.4 · 2The trade-off: larger n captures longer dependencies but makes the data sparser — most n-grams in the table have never been seen, so more of the probability mass sits on counts of zero.
  • 7.5 · 2An n-gram is a sequence of n consecutive words; the model order is what varies, not the definition. A bigram model scores a sentence with n−1 bigrams.

A sentence probability is a product along a chain Each arc carries one conditional bigram probability. Multiplying them gives the probability of the whole sentence — the Chain Rule plus the bigram Markov assumption, nothing more. The chain is folded onto two lines here only to keep it readable.

If any single arc is 0 the whole sentence scores 0 — exactly the problem Laplace smoothing exists to fix.

06

Laplace (add-one) smoothing

Why it exists, how it works, and the formula — the recording asks for all three.

考这题就写三件事

录音里明确问了三问：定义、为什么需要、公式。答的时候按这个顺序写，一段一个关键词：

  1. 为什么：训练集里没出现的 bigram 计数是 0，概率就是 0；句子概率是连乘，一个 0 就让 整句变成 0，perplexity 也就没法算了（不能除以 0）。这叫 zero-probability problem / data sparsity。
  2. 怎么做：假装每个 bigram 都多见过一次，把所有计数 +1；为了仍然是合法概率分布， 分母要加上词汇量 V。
  3. 公式：P*(wn|wn−1) = (C(wn−1wn)+1) / (C(wn−1)+V)。 分母加的是 V，不是 1，这是最常见的错。

加分点：说明 add-1 对 n-gram 来说是"钝器"（blunt instrument），因为它把太多概率质量分给了 没出现过的组合；实际中更多用 interpolation 或 backoff。但老师只要求 add-1，写这一句是加分， 不写也不扣。

Model answer — "What is Laplace smoothing? Why is it needed, and what is the formula?"

Markszero problem · add one to every count · denominator +V · never zero · V = vocabulary size

  • 8.1 · 2The problem: a bigram that never occurs in training has C = 0, so its MLE probability is 0. Because a sentence probability is a product, a single zero makes the whole sentence score 0 — and perplexity becomes undefined.

Word embeddings and cosine similarity

The recording asks for the idea, the objective function, and a cosine similarity computation from a given word-word matrix.

余弦相似度：先算三个数，再相除

题目给你一个"词-词矩阵"（word-word matrix / co-occurrence matrix），让你算两个词的余弦相似度。 按这个顺序写，步骤分就拿到了：

  1. 从矩阵里各取一行作为两个向量 a 和 b。 矩阵的每一行就是一个词的向量。
  2. 算点积 a·b = 对应位置相乘再相加。
  3. 算两个模长 ‖a‖ = √(Σai²)，‖b‖ 同理。
  4. 相除：cos = a·b / (‖a‖·‖b‖)。结果一定在 0 到 1 之间 （词频矩阵没有负数）。

自查：两个向量完全相同时结果必须正好是 1；如果算出大于 1，一定是模长算错了。 另外要写一句解释：值越接近 1，说明这两个词出现的上下文越像，语义越接近。

Cosine similarity is the angle between two word vectors Only the direction matters, not the length — which is why cosine is preferred to Euclidean distance when the words have very different frequencies.

Range is −1 to 1. For word-count vectors, whose entries are all ≥ 0, it is 0 to 1.

Why vectors at all — one line of motivation

One-hot vectors make every pair of distinct words orthogonal: motel and hotel have cosine similarity exactly 0 (verified), so a search for Seattle motel cannot match a page about Seattle hotel. Hand-built resources such as WordNet patch this over but miss nuance, miss new meanings, and need human labour. The fix is to learn to encode similarity in the vectors themselves.

Model answer — "What are word embeddings, and what is the objective function?"

Marksdense vector · distributional semantics · centre vs context vector · softmax · minimise J(θ) · SG vs CBOW

  • 9.1 · 2A word embedding (word vector / word representation) is a dense vector of real numbers for each word, chosen so that it is similar to the vectors of words appearing in similar contexts — banking = [0.286, 0.792, −0.177, −0.107, 0.109, −0.542, 0.349, 0.271]. It is a distributed representation, not a one-hot code.
  • 9.2 · 2The concept is distributional semantics: a word's meaning is given by the words that frequently appear close by — "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" (Firth 1957). The context is the set of words inside a fixed-size window around the target.
  • 9.3 · 2Word2vec (Mikolov et al. 2013) learns them by walking through every position t, taking a centre word c and its context ("outside") words o, using the similarity of their vectors to compute the probability of o given c, and adjusting the vectors to maximise that probability. Each word holds two vectors — vw as a centre word and uw as a context word.
  • 9.4 · 3Prediction function — a softmax over the whole vocabulary:
    P(o | c) = exp(uoTvc) / Σw ∈ V exp(uwTvc). The denominator runs over all V words, which is exactly why the objective is expensive.
  • 9.5 · 3Objective function — predict the context words within a window of size m at every position, and take the negative log likelihood over the corpus:
    J(θ) = −(1/T) Σt=1T Σ−m ≤ j ≤ m, j ≠ 0 log P(wt+j | wt).
    We minimise J(θ). The leading minus sign is the part most often dropped — minimising the negative log likelihood is maximising the probability of the observed context words.
  • 9.6 · 2Two variants: Skip-gram predicts context words from the centre word; CBOW predicts the centre word from the bag of context words. Negative sampling is the standard efficiency trick, since the softmax denominator above touches every word in V for every window.
  • 9.7 · 2Count-based alternative: build a word-word co-occurrence matrix, reweight the cells (e.g. PPMI), then reduce dimension with SVD. The matrix in the cosine question below is a small version of this route.

A Markov model (Markov chain) is a finite state machine with probabilistic state transitions: the next state depends only on the current state, not on the history before it. The materials — what an HMM is made of

Markshidden states · s0 and sF · observation vocabulary · A · B · λ = {A, B} · rows sum to 1

  • 12.1 · 2A hidden Markov model is a probabilistic generative model for sequences. There is an underlying set of hidden (unobserved, latent) states; the model makes probabilistic transitions between them over time; and it probabilistically generates a token from whatever state it is in. You only ever see the generated tokens, never the states.
  • 12.2 · 3The materials — quote the formal definition:
    • a set of N + 2 states S = {s0, s1, …, sN, sF}, with a distinguished start state s0 and a distinguished final state sF;
    • a set of M possible observations V = {v1, …, vM} (the vocabulary);
    • a state transition probability distribution A = {aij}, aij = P(next state sj | current state si);
    • an observation (emission) probability distribution for each state j, B = {bj(k)}, bj(k) = P(emit observation vk | state sj);
    • the whole parameter set is λ = {A, B}.
    Note there is no separate initial-distribution vector: the initial distribution is carried by the transitions out of the start state s0, i.e. a0j.
  • 12.3 · 2Every row of A and of B sums to 1: Σj aij = 1 for every i, Σk bj(k) = 1 for every j. Checking this is a free sanity test in the exam.
  • 12.4 · 2Two assumptions make it tractable: the Markov assumption, that the next state depends only on the current state, and the output independence assumption, that the observation depends only on the state that emitted it.
  • 12.5 · 2The NLP reading: the states are part-of-speech tags (Det, Noun, PropNoun, Verb) and the observations are words (the, a, cat, dog, bit, ate). Tagging a sentence is then "find the most likely state sequence" — computation 2 below.
定义这题必须写对的三件事
  1. 状态集写成 S = {s₀, s₁, …, s_N, s_F}，点明 s₀ 是起始状态、 s_F 是终止状态。这两个是课件专门标出来的，写了才有分。
  2. 参数集写 λ = {A, B}，不要写 π。课件把初始分布放在 s₀ 的出边上（a₀ⱼ），没有单独的 π 向量。
  3. A 是状态转移概率，B 是发射（观测）概率， 两个矩阵每一行都要加起来等于 1。

如果考卷上给的是带 π 的版本，那就是把 π 当作 s₀ 的出边，两者等价，照算即可。

Computation 3 — estimating the parameters, three ways

(a) Supervised — every training sequence is completely labelled (tagged)

With the state sequence annotated, all of λ = {A, B} can be estimated directly:

aij = C(i → j) / C(i)from tag-bigram and tag-unigram counts
bj(o) = C(o, j) / C(j)from tag/word co-occurrence counts
a(s0, j) = C(start at j) / number of sequencesthe start-state row

Add the lecture's own closing line: use appropriate smoothing if the training data is sparse — which is precisely the Laplace smoothing of topic 6, applied here to A and B.

(b) Unsupervised — Baum-Welch (EM)

With no labels you cannot count, so you count in expectation. Run forward and backward to get α and β, then:

γt(i) = αt(i)·βt(i) / P(O)P(state i at time t)
ξt(i,j) = αt(i)·aij·bj(ot+1)·βt+1(j) / P(O)P(i→j at t)
â(s0, j) = γ₁(j)the start-state row
âij = Σt ξt(i,j) / Σt γt(i)expected transitions
b̂j(o) = Σt: ot=o γt(j) / Σt γt(j)expected emissions

Iterate until the likelihood stops rising. EM guarantees the likelihood never decreases — a useful check, and a fact worth quoting. Note it finds a local optimum, so the starting guess matters.

(c) Semi-supervised — labels for part of the data

Use the labelled portion to get exact counts (method a), then treat the unlabelled portion as Baum-Welch's expected counts (method b), add the two, and normalise once at the end. The normalisation step is the same division as in the supervised case; that is why the semi-supervised version needs no new formula.

Supervisedhard counts C(i→j), C(o,j)
Unsupervisedexpected counts Σξ, Σγ from forward-backward
Semi-supervisedhard counts + expected counts, added then normalised

  • Supervised：需要带标注的语料（每个词都标了词性）。直接数数相除。局限：标注贵。
  • Unsupervised：只需要原始文本。用 Baum-Welch（EM），先跑 forward-backward 得到 γ 和 ξ，再用期望计数重新估参数，反复迭代。 局限：只能收敛到局部最优，起始值有影响。
  • Semi-supervised：一部分有标注。有标注的按第一种种数数，没标注的按第二种种算期望计数， 两个加起来再统一归一化。局限：需要设计好两边怎么加权。

QUESTION 2 · 8 marks

Write a regular expression for each of the following: (a) any word ending in ing; (b) a date in DD/MM/YYYY form; (c) a hexadecimal colour code #RRGGBB; (d) a capitalised word.

Show answer

Marksword boundaries · exact counts · character classes · anchors

  • 2.1 · 2(a) \b\w+ing\b — matches running, sing; rejects kingdom, and rejects ing itself because \w+ needs at least one character before it.
  • 2.2 · 2(b) \b\d{2}/\d{2}/\d{4}\b. Exact counts, because \d+ would also accept 1/1/26.
  • 2.3 · 2(c) #[0-9A-Fa-f]{6}\b — six hex digits, not +, so #FFF is rejected.
  • 2.4 · 2(d) \b[A-Z][a-z]+\b — a capital followed by one or more lower-case letters; rejects MACAO and cat.

QUESTION 3 · 8 marks

(a) Draw the finite state automaton for /ab*c/. (b) Give the regular expression recognised by an automaton that accepts abc, ababc, abababc but not ac or abbc. State how many states your machine needs.

Show answer

Marksone state per required symbol · loop placement · double ring · reading back

  • 3.1 · 4(a) a b* c → q0 —a→ q1, where q1 carries a self-loop on b, then q1 —c→ q2 (double ring). The loop is on the state reached after the a, and * means zero or more, so ac is accepted as well as abc and abbc.
  • 3.2 · 4(b) /(ab)+c/. Reading it back off a diagram: the loop covers two symbols (a then b), so it must be drawn as an arc from the state after ab back to the state before a — four states plus the loop arc. The + guarantees at least one ab, so ac is rejected; abbc is rejected because the loop only fires on a whole ab.

QUESTION 4 · 10 marks

A corpus of three sentences, |V| = 10:

<s> the cat sat on the mat </s>
<s> the dog sat on the mat </s>
<s> the cat ate the fish </s>

Counts: C(the) = 6, C(the cat) = 2, C(the dog) = 1, C(the mat) = 2, C(sat) = 2, C(cat sat) = 1, C(cat) = 2.

(a) Give P(cat | the) and P(sat | cat). (b) Apply Laplace smoothing to both, showing the denominator. (c) Compute P(<s> the cat sat on the mat </s>) from the raw counts. (d) Why can the smoothed version never be zero?

Show answer

MarksMLE ÷ C(first word) · Laplace ÷ (C+V) · sentence product · the zero argument

  • 4.1 · 2(a) P(cat | the) = C(the cat)/C(the) = 2/6 = 0.3333. P(sat | cat) = C(cat sat)/C(cat) = 1/2 = 0.5.
  • 4.2 · 3(b) Laplace with V = 10: P*(cat | the) = (2+1)/(6+10) = 3/16 = 0.1875; P*(sat | cat) = (1+1)/(2+10) = 2/12 = 0.1667. The denominator gains V, not 1.
  • 4.3 · 3(c) P(the|<s>)·P(cat|the)·P(sat|cat)·P(on|sat)·P(the|on)·P(mat|the)·P(</s>|mat) = (3/3) × (2/6) × (1/2) × (2/2) × (2/2) × (2/6) × (2/2) = 0.05556.
  • 4.4 · 2(d) Every numerator is C+1, so no factor can be 0, and every denominator is finite and positive — the product is therefore strictly positive for any string. That is exactly the point: a single zero in the raw product would force the whole sentence probability to 0 and make perplexity undefined.

The start-state transitions are a(s0,A) = 0.6 and a(s0,B) = 0.4. The observation sequence is x y.

(a) Compute P(x y) using the forward algorithm. (b) Find the most likely state sequence with Viterbi. (c) Verify your answer to (a) by brute force over all state sequences. (d) Name the three ways of estimating the parameters, and say which one needs labelled data.

QUESTION 7 · 6 marks

(a) Explain the difference between lemmatization and stemming, with one example each. (b) Give the lemma and a plausible stem for studies and better. (c) Name the three categories of harm in text classification and give one example of each.

Show answer

Marksreal word vs chopped · POS/dictionary · worked pairs · 3 named harms with examples

  • 7.1 · 2(a) Lemmatization returns the dictionary headword and needs a lexicon plus the part of speech (are → be); stemming chops affixes by rule and the result need not be a word (happiness → happi). Stemming is faster, lemmatization is more accurate.
  • 7.2 · 2(b) studies → lemma study, stem studi (the rule just strips the s). better → lemma good (irregular, dictionary needed); a stemmer leaves it as better because no suffix rule fires.
  • 7.3 · 2(c) Representational harms — sentiment systems score sentences with African-American names more negatively (Kiritchenko & Mohammad 2018). Harms of censorship — toxicity detectors flag non-toxic sentences that merely mention "blind" or "gay", silencing those groups. Performance disparities — language identification is less accurate for African-American or Indian writers of English.
RECAP

Last hour

Everything on this page, on one screen. Read this in the queue outside the hall.

Text, tokens & patterns

  • Types vs instances. N = running total with repeats. |V| = distinct forms. LDS = |V|/N. Heaps: |V| = kNβ, 0.67 < β < 0.75.
  • Space tokenization splits on whitespace. Fails on punctuation, clitics (we're), multiword names, and Chinese/Japanese/Thai.
  • Lemma = dictionary word, needs POS + a lexicon (better → good). Stem = chopped, may not be a word (studies → studi).
  • Regex: ? * + bind to one preceding character. Use \b or ^…$ or you match inside words. \d{2} for exactly two digits.
  • FSA = 5-tuple (Q, Σ, q0, F, δ). Regex ↔ FSA both directions; same regular language. +/* → self-loop, ? → bypass arc, | → branch. Double ring = accepting.

Language models & probability

  • Bigram MLE: P(wn|wn−1) = C(wn−1wn) / C(wn−1). Divide by the first word's count only.
  • Laplace: (C+1) / (C(wn−1) + V). The denominator gains V, not 1. It exists because one zero makes a whole sentence probability zero.
  • Cosine: a·b / (‖a‖ · ‖b‖). Range 0…1 for count vectors. Identical vectors → exactly 1. High cosine = similar contexts = similar meaning.
  • Embeddings objective: push real (word, context) pairs together with log P(o | c) where P(o|c) = exp(uoTvc) / Σwexp(uwTvc), and minimise J(θ) = −(1/T) Σt Σj≠0 log P(wt+j|wt).
  • Classification harms: representational · censorship · performance disparities. Causes: data, labels, objective. No general fix.

Hidden Markov models & wrap-up

  • HMM materials: S = {s0, s1…sN, sF} · observations V · A · B · λ = {A, B} (no separate π — the start state carries it). Every row of A and B sums to 1.
  • Forward uses Σ, gives P(observations); sum only the last column. Viterbi uses max + backpointers, gives the best path. Viterbi ≤ forward, always.
  • Supervised = count and divide. Unsupervised = Baum-Welch EM with expected counts γ and ξ; likelihood never decreases. Semi-supervised = both counts added, then normalised once.
  • Always finish with a sanity check: probabilities in [0,1], rows sum to 1, cosine ≤ 1, Viterbi ≤ forward.
